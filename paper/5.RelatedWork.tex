\section{Related Work}

\noindent\textbf{Quantum Simulation and NISQ Systems.}
Computer architecture uses measurement and modeling to study immature systems~\cite{skadron2003challenges}; \SystemName applies that discipline to quantum advantage. ScaleQsim, AURORA-Q, and \cuQuantum improve state-vector simulation through distribution, GPUs, tiered memory, asynchronous movement, and optimized primitives~\cite{scaleqsim,auroraq,cuquantum}. Benchmark suites such as SuperMarQ, QASMBench, MQT Bench, QAOAKit, QED-C, and QCHALLenge provide circuits, metrics, parameters, and application-aware tests~\cite{supermarq,qasmbench,mqtbench,qaoakit,qedcbench,qchallenge}. These works are essential measurement substrates, but they do not by themselves compare the full quantum-circuit application path with a same-input native HPC deadline and quality target.

\noindent\textbf{Quantum Applications and Native Baselines.}
Quantum kernels, variational classifiers, VQE chemistry, QAOA optimization, and Hamiltonian simulation are common advantage candidates~\cite{biamonte2017quantum,cerezo2022challenges,kandala2017hardware,farhi2014qaoa,georgescu2014quantum,daley2022practical}. Each domain also has strong native baselines, from classical ML libraries to exact, sparse, heuristic, and Krylov-style solvers~\cite{scikit-learn}. Fault-tolerant and resource-estimation work models code distance, cycle time, layout, magic-state factories, decoder latency, and physical assumptions~\cite{fowler2012surface,litinski2019game,azureestimator,webber2022impact,googleqec2025belowthreshold}; QHPC and hybrid-runtime studies add gateway interfaces, queueing, fidelity, scheduling, and classical-resource constraints~\cite{ornlbridging2026,ornlhpcecosystem2024,qfwsc25,nerscquantumroadmap2025,qurator,k8squantum}. \SystemName is complementary: it supplies same-task native baselines, measured quality gaps, repeated-evaluation counts, circuit metadata, and shot/control sensitivity artifacts that those estimators and runtimes can plug into.
