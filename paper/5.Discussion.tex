\section{Discussion and Future Work}

\noindent\textbf{Architecture Meaning of the Frontier:}
\SystemName does not prove current quantum advantage. It turns advantage into a computer-architecture boundary: once task, quality target, and native baseline are fixed, the remaining gap can be assigned to logical execution, shot parallelism, decoder/control overhead, native-baseline strength, or quantum algorithm quality. The output is a workload-specific target for what a future QPU must change before the full quantum-circuit application beats the native HPC path.

\noindent\textbf{Future QPU Control Stack:}
The main implication is that a single ``faster quantum computer'' roadmap is too coarse. Surface-code demonstrations emphasize decoding and cycle time~\cite{googleqec2025belowthreshold}, LDPC proposals trade qubit overhead against connectivity and decoder requirements~\cite{bravyi2024qldpcmemory}, and FTQC roadmaps include modularity, magic-state resources, and control hardware~\cite{ibmftqcroadmap2025}. Our results map those choices to applications: ML and Opt. need quality-preserving encodings, batched submission, and low-latency feedback; Chem exposes decoder bandwidth, measurement aggregation, and observable batching; Sim. points toward Hamiltonian-simulation-oriented analog/digital execution. Annealing can help optimization cases that embed cleanly as energy minimization~\cite{dwaveannealingdocs}, but it is not a general replacement for the gate-model paths measured here.

\noindent\textbf{HPC--Quantum Integrated Architecture:}
QHPC systems should treat a QPU as an accelerator inside a scientific computing workflow, not as a detached queue. \SystemName supplies the quantitative contract such systems need: native deadlines, quality tolerances, circuit-evaluation counts, shot-parallel regions, and serial decoder/control floors. These signals let an integrated runtime co-schedule CPU/GPU kernels, simulator backends, QPU batches, measurements, optimizers, and quality checks as one workflow.

\noindent\textbf{Deployment-Scale Proxy Boundary:}
Exact state-vector execution cannot simply be pushed to deployment-scale molecules, graphs, or Hamiltonians by spending more GPU node-hours. The useful next step is a proxy record: keep the native deadline, circuit metadata, quality target, and projection knobs, while replacing full state-vector execution with domain solvers, tensor/approximate checks, or metadata-only circuit construction when exact simulation becomes the bottleneck. \noindent\textbf{Scope and Future Work:}
The largest modeling risk is an underpowered native baseline. Stronger natives on larger tasks, including deeper CNN/ResNet families, boosted trees, tuned MILP/metaheuristics, tensor networks, projector QMC, and CCSD(T)-class references, can still move the frontier rightward; the method quantifies how much. \cuQuantum is instrumentation, not the future hardware stack. Hardware-specific studies should replace $T_{\mathrm{error}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{shots}}$ with code distance, cycle time, logical-error budget, magic-state throughput, decoder latency, controller bandwidth, and device concurrency. Future versions should attach energy and mitigation cost by comparing $E_{\mathrm{native}}=N_{\mathrm{GPU}}P_{\mathrm{GPU}}T_{\mathrm{native}}$ with refrigerator, decoder, control, host, and error-mitigation overhead over $T_{\mathrm{qhw}}$. Bundled Slurm runs, 12 warmup-separated trials, zero failures, maximum quantum-runtime CV is 0.0400, and artifact audits reduce timing and provenance risk.
