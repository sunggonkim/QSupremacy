\section{Conclusion}

This paper presents \SystemName, a Perlmutter-based measurement and modeling framework for practical quantum supremacy claims. The central claim is that practical quantum advantage should be treated as an application-level break-even condition under the same input and quality target. On \Perlmutter, the controlled digits sweep shows that quantum kernels require a median 421.9$\times$ projected speedup to match native time, while QNN/VQC requires 64.9$\times$ but often misses native quality. The practical suite covers ML, chemistry, optimization, and simulation, and the large profile scales to 3,552 cases on 32 GPU nodes.

The main lesson is that no single quantum-speedup number is meaningful across applications. Simulation is the closest family in our measured suite: with 90\% quality-gap recovery, a $10^4\times$ projected execution improvement covers 54.9\% of simulation cases. Chemistry needs a larger runtime shift: $10^5\times$ covers 57.1\% of chemistry cases at the same recovery level. ML and optimization are different: faster hardware alone does not rescue the current quantum configurations unless the quality gap also closes. Thus, the output of a practical quantum-supremacy study should be a frontier and a bottleneck taxonomy, not a yes/no slogan.

These results do not show quantum advantage today. They show that HPC-based quantum simulation is valuable as an instrumentation layer for a sharper systems question: which workloads are speed-limited, which are quality-limited, and what hardware and algorithmic changes are required before a quantum path can beat a strong native HPC baseline.
